## Research

My work asks how legislative and policy questions can be studied as complex, dynamic social systems. I combine legal analysis with data-driven empirical methods and generative-agent modelling.

<div class="research-grid" markdown="0">
<div class="research-card"><div class="research-body"><h4 class="research-title">Legislation and institutional design</h4><p class="research-desc">I examine the constitutional basis, institutional mechanisms, and implementation logic of legislation, with particular attention to how legal rules shape social coordination and public outcomes.</p></div></div>
<div class="research-card"><div class="research-body"><h4 class="research-title">Generative-agent social simulation</h4><p class="research-desc">I use large language models as theory-informed cognitive cores for heterogeneous social agents, integrating memory, emotion, perception, and decision-making into reproducible simulation systems.</p></div></div>
<div class="research-card"><div class="research-body"><h4 class="research-title">Legislation and policy evaluation</h4><p class="research-desc">I develop methods that link scenario-based policy experiments to observed data and statistical validation, enabling comparison, robustness assessment, and design optimization.</p></div></div>
</div>

### GPLab framework

The framework is organized around three connected layers: social agents, modular social subsystems, and simulation and evaluation. This architecture supports policy-specific scenario design without losing the capacity to study cross-domain feedback and heterogeneous behavioral response.
