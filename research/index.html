Research

My work asks how legislative and policy questions can be studied as complex, dynamic social systems. I combine legal analysis with data-driven empirical methods and generative-agent modelling.

Legislation and institutional design

I examine the constitutional basis, institutional mechanisms, and implementation logic of legislation, with particular attention to how legal rules shape social coordination and public outcomes.

Generative-agent social simulation

I use large language models as theory-informed cognitive cores for heterogeneous social agents, integrating memory, emotion, perception, and decision-making into reproducible simulation systems.

Legislation and policy evaluation

I develop methods that link scenario-based policy experiments to observed data and statistical validation, enabling comparison, robustness assessment, and design optimization.

GPLab framework

The original framework figure published in Zhang, Peng, and Ren (2026), Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation. Read the article.

The framework is organized around three connected layers: social agents, modular social subsystems, and simulation and evaluation. This architecture supports policy-specific scenario design without losing the capacity to study cross-domain feedback and heterogeneous behavioral response.
